## Figure 2B (replacement panel) — `Figure2B_dual_coding_potential_vs_selection.pdf`

**B. Dual-coding potential is near-universal in coding exons; selection on the alternate
frame is what separates DCRs from it.**
**(i)** Percentage of exons containing at least one dual-coding-capable interval, i.e. a
sub-interval of at least 21 nt that is open in two forward reading frames (permissive
sub-interval rule). Human DCR exons, 98.0% (n = 3,324; error bar, Wilson 95% CI 97.5–98.4%).
Non-DCR coding exons, 94.1%: the fraction of non-DCR coding exons containing at least one
latent dual-coding interval (289,416 of 307,523 exons). This object is the whole exon, not the
DCR itself. Potential alone carries almost no information about function: of all exons with
this potential, the fraction that are not DCRs is X = N/(N + D) = 0.9940 (N = 289,416
non-DCR exons; D = 1,741 DCR intervals). [definition_reconciliation.csv; xprime_summary.csv;
non-DCR exon total from gc_audit_summary.csv]
**(ii)** Percentage of intervals still dual-coding in the mouse ortholog (liftOver to mm39,
strict rule). DCRs, 81.5%; latent dual-coding intervals from non-DCR exons, length-matched,
77.2%; all latent intervals (not length-matched), 69.9%. Conserving the potential narrows it
only slightly. Bracket, the length-matched gap: 4.3 percentage points (DCR/length-matched
ratio 1.056, 95% CI 1.033–1.081; Mantel–Haenszel OR = 1.35; Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test
across length strata, p = 1.7 × 10⁻⁵). [xprime_summary.csv]
**(iii)** Alternate-frame selection, ω_alt = dSN/dSS: the substitution rate at sites that are
non-synonymous in the alternate frame but synonymous in the reference frame, relative to sites
synonymous in both. Restricting to reference-synonymous sites removes the constraint imposed by
the reference frame by construction. Estimator after Nelson et al. (2020; OLGenie); Tress et
al. [2026] independently apply the same logic (their dS/dS strategy). Intervals on
non-primary contigs (_alt, _random, _fix, chrUn) are excluded from both sets. DCRs (SwissProt
protein frames), ω_alt = 0.443 (95% CI 0.372–0.522; n = 1,215 intervals ≥ 30 nt); latent
null (non-DCR intervals with dual-coding potential), ω_alt = 0.988 (95% CI 0.955–1.024;
n = 19,948). Dotted line, ω = 1 (neutral). Error bars, bootstrap 95% CI over intervals
(2,000 replicates). Bracket, two-sided bootstrap test of the difference in ω_alt between
sets: none of the 2,000 replicates crossed zero, p < 0.0005. [selection_omega_v3.csv,
primary_contigs_only (v3), main comparison]

*Note for authors (not legend text):* the 94.1% is computed in the script as N_total
(xprime_summary.csv) over the 21,031 + 286,492 non-DCR coding exons of gc_audit_summary.csv.
An independent exon-level recount (figures/verify_latent_exon_fraction.py) gives
288,930–289,067 of 307,523 exons (93.95–94.00%), consistent with N being an exon count.

## Supplementary figure — GC-content controls (R2-M1) — `Supp_GC_content_controls.pdf`

**GC content does not explain the dual-coding or conservation properties of DCRs.**
**A.** Per-exon GC content of human DCR-overlapping exons (D, n = 3,324), non-DCR exons of DCR
genes (D*, n = 21,031) and non-DCR exons of non-DCR genes (ND, n = 286,492), computed with
bedtools nuc on hg38. Medians are 54.23%, 51.47% and 51.22%. DCR exons are slightly GC-richer
than both comparators (median difference 2.76 and 3.01 percentage points; two-sided
Mann–Whitney U, p = 6.6 × 10⁻²⁵ and 5.6 × 10⁻²⁸), but the effect is small (rank-biserial
r = −0.11 for both).
**B.** GC content of the mouse orthologs of human DCRs (n = 1,603) and of random mouse
control intervals (n = 100,000): medians 53.49% vs 52.31%, Mann–Whitney p = 3.1 × 10⁻⁵,
rank-biserial r = −0.061.
**C.** Percentage of mouse regions that are dual-coding (≥2 full-length ORFs): mouse DCR
orthologs, 82.6%, against random mouse control intervals matched on length only (60.5%) or on
GC content and length (64.8%; error bar, 95% resampling interval 62.9–66.8%). Matching on GC
closes only 4.3 of the 22.1 percentage-point gap.
**D.** Composition null: each mouse DCR ortholog's own codons are shuffled, so GC and codon
composition are preserved exactly and only codon arrangement changes. The observed count of
dual-coding orthologs (1,318; 82.4%) is far above the self-shuffle null (mean 1,096, 68.6%;
error bar, 95% interval of the null, 1,067–1,124; 1,000 shuffles; Z = 15.5; empirical
p = 0.001, the minimum attainable with 1,000 shuffles). The 13.8-point excess therefore comes
from codon arrangement, not composition. Panel D uses the observed value recomputed from
sequence for the self-shuffle (1,318/1,599). It differs from the published 82.6%
(1,319/1,596, pipeline table; panel C) by 0.2 percentage points because of 3 orthologs.
**E.** Mean phyloP (100-way) of DCR exons (D) and of non-DCR exons (D*, ND), before
(baseline) and after GC- and length-matched resampling of the controls. DCR exons stay less
conserved than matched controls (2.602 vs 2.729 for D*, Mann–Whitney p = 1.3 × 10⁻³; vs 2.811
for ND, p = 2.8 × 10⁻⁷). In a linear model meanPP ~ is_DCR + GC + length, the DCR coefficient
is −0.194 (p = 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁰) independently of GC (−0.039 per GC percentage point).
Significance: *** p < 0.001, ** p < 0.01. [gc_audit_summary.csv, gc_audit_tests.csv,
gc_matched_fig2b.csv, self_shuffle_control.csv, phyloP_gc_control.csv; per-exon GC
regenerated from the DOOVER beds by make_gc_tables.py, which reproduces every audited median]

*Note for authors:* the 1,000 null replicates behind panel D were not saved with the imported
results, so D shows the null as mean ± 95% interval, not as a histogram.

## Supplementary figure — splice sites, expression class, tissue specificity —
`Supp_splice_site_expression_tissue_specificity.pdf`

**Splice-site conservation, expression class and tissue specificity of DCR regions compared
with controls.**
**A.** phyloP (100-way) at the splice sites flanking DCR exons and control exons, split by
junction (upstream/downstream of the exon) and site (donor/acceptor). Boxes, interquartile
range; centre line, median; whiskers, 1.5 × IQR; outliers not drawn. n (DCR / control):
upstream donor 3,076 / 13,998; upstream acceptor 3,076 / 13,408; downstream acceptor 1,520 /
14,131; downstream donor 1,520 / 13,303. DCR splice sites are slightly less conserved than control
splice sites in all four classes (Bonferroni-adjusted p 6.3 × 10⁻⁴ to 0.031; two-sided
Mann–Whitney U, five tests), with negligible effect sizes (|rank-biserial| ≤ 0.05; median
difference −0.06 to −0.11 phyloP units). Per class, p_adj = 6.3 × 10⁻⁴ (upstream donor),
0.031 (upstream acceptor), 0.013 (downstream acceptor) and 0.016 (downstream donor).
**B.** Percentage of genes carrying a DCR in each GTEx v8 expression quintile (median TPM
across 54 tissue sites; 16,532 genes with mapped SwissProt isoforms, 1,230 of them DCR
genes). Error bars, 95% CI. Dashed line, overall rate (7.44%). DCR incidence does not change
with expression level (logistic regression on log₁₀(TPM + 1): OR = 1.039, p = 0.41).
**C.** Tissue specificity (Yanai τ) of DCR genes vs non-DCR genes within each expression
quintile. No quintile differs (Mann–Whitney p = 0.26, 0.58, 0.76, 0.41 and 0.35; ns). Across
all genes, DCR genes are marginally *less* tissue-specific (median τ 0.7765 vs 0.7952,
rank-biserial 0.042). This is a gene-level result. It does not bear on Figure 4, which
concerns which reading frame dominates within a tissue, not whether the gene is
tissue-restricted. [splice_site_phylop.tsv, splice_site_conservation.csv,
A4_dcr_by_expression.csv, A4_gene_expression_tau.csv, A4_tau_by_expression.csv; the
logistic-regression and overall τ values are from A4_results.md]

## Supplementary figure — GNAS (replaces "data not shown") —
`Supp_GNAS_DCR_gene_model_mouse_ORFs.pdf`

**GNAS DCR 201 is the only DCR longer than 500 nt that is conserved as dual-coding in mouse,
and the mouse ortholog carries two full-length ORFs.**
**A.** Human GNAS SwissProt isoform models, mapped to hg38 with MIRAGE2 (DOOVER isoform BED12),
drawn in the style of Figure 2E: NESP55 (O95467-1), XLαs (Q5JWF2-1, -2, -3), ALEX (P84996-1) and
Gαs (P63092-1 to -4). The triangle and black frame mark DCR 201 (chr20:58,853,453–58,855,330,
plus strand, 1,878 nt), which is translated in two frames. Zoom: ALEX (P84996-1) reads it in
the frame that the pipeline labels canonical (offset 0); the XLαs isoforms (Q5JWF2-1 to -3)
read it in the alternative frame (offset +2). The yellow box marks the DCR interval. Frame
offset = position (nt) of the first complete codon inside the DCR, in transcript direction;
this convention gives the "+2"/"0" labels of Figure 2E. Exon reading frames are derived from
the SwissProt protein models, not from GENCODE CDS annotation. A second, 30-nt GNAS DCR
(DCR 202, chr20:58,895,612–58,895,641) lies in the Gαs/XLαs-shared region and is not
highlighted.
**B.** The mouse ortholog of DCR 201 (liftOver to mm39, chr2:174,139,854–174,142,007, plus
strand, 2,154 nt), translated in the three forward frames. Frame 1 (718 aa) and frame 3
(717 aa) are open over the whole interval. Frame 2 contains 29 stop codons (black ticks).
Mouse frames are assigned to human frames by peptide homology, not by position, and coloured
accordingly: frame 1 shares 20.1% of its
4-mer peptides with the human ALEX-frame translation (2.2% with XLαs), and frame 3 shares
22.9% with the XLαs frame (3.0% with ALEX). Both human reading frames are therefore
conserved as full-length ORFs in mouse. Of the three genes with a DCR > 500 nt (GBGT1,
ZNF586, GNAS), GBGT1's mouse ortholog is not dual-coding and ZNF586's DCR does not map to
mouse. [gnas_dcr_gt500.csv; DOOVER BED12 and New_TableS1.tsv; gnas_mouse_summary.csv from
prep_gene_models.py]

## Supplementary figure — GABBR1 (R2-m1) — `Supp_GABBR1_DCR_gene_model.pdf`

**GABBR1 DCR 1812, a 26-nt dual-coding region at the 3′ end of isoform Q9UBS5-5.**
**A.** The five SwissProt GABBR1 isoforms (Q9UBS5-1 to -5) as mapped by MIRAGE2 (DOOVER
isoform BED12), drawn in the style of Figure 2E. The gene is on the minus strand, so
transcription runs right to left (chevrons). The DCR lies on **chr6_GL000255v2_alt**, an
alternate-haplotype contig of the MHC region in GRCh38, not on the primary chr6 assembly:
chr6_GL000255v2_alt:874,220–874,245 (1-based; BED 874219–874245). Zoom: Q9UBS5-1 to -4 read the
DCR as part of a 132-nt internal exon in the canonical frame (frame offset +1). In Q9UBS5-5
(UniProt "isoform 1E") the same 26 nt form a shorter terminal exon, read in the alternative
frame (offset +2) up to the C-terminus. The yellow box marks the DCR interval; the frame
offset convention is as in Figure 2E.
**B.** DCR 1812 in transcript orientation (5′→3′; genomic coordinates decreasing from
874,245 to 874,220) and its translation in both frames. Canonical frame: YIQNSQPN
(Q9UBS5-1 to -4). Alternative frame: ISRTHSPT, the C-terminal eight residues of Q9UBS5-5.
Both peptides match the UniProt sequences.
SwissProt evidence (DOOVER New_TableS1.tsv): the canonical isoform Q9UBS5-1 has
protein-level evidence. The alternative-frame isoform Q9UBS5-5 carries ECO:0000269
(experimental evidence) and ECO:0000305. Q9UBS5-2, -3 and -4, which use the canonical frame,
carry ECO:0000303, ECO:0000303 and ECO:0000305, respectively. At 26 nt the DCR is below the
30-nt minimum of the alternate-frame selection test (Fig. 2B(iii)), so that test does not
cover it.
